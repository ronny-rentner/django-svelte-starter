# Visa FAQ

## When should I book the appointment at the embassy to start my visa process?
Please book the appointment at your local German embassy as early as possible in your process. Often you have waiting periods of several weeks or months. If the appointment can’t take place due to external circumstances you can cancel it any time, but you can often not book it on short notice.

## What kind of visa should I choose for the appointment at the embassy?
Please go for an appointment for a national visa for a stay for more than 90 days – this is the visa category type D in general. The reason you choose depends on your reason to immigrate. It can be either work, family reunion or study purpose.

## What are the specific requirements for a so-called blue card?
To obtain a blue card you need to fulfill some requirements: (I.) your degree needs to be acknowledged in Germany, which you can proof when it is listed in the official database Anabin, (II.) the position you want to start in needs to be related to your degree and (III.) your annual salary needs to be either at least EUR 45.300 (in 2024) or between EUR 41.041,80 and 45.300 (in 2024) if it is for a position where Germany faces a shortage of employees, mostly in MINT positions. 

In some professional areas a blue card can be granted as well when you don’t have a university degree and work experience can be shown instead.
https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa/kinds-of-visa/eu-blue-card/

## What are the advantages of a so-called blue card?
A blue card is a specific residence title which is baked on a Directive from the European Union and exists in several EU member states - the German version has two main advantages:
you can apply for a permanent residence title faster than with other residence titles, after 27 months and with really good German skills on level B1 after 21 months already.
https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__18c.html
In addition, you can bring your family and there is no need for them to show any German language skills - like for all residence titles which are employment related. You can even bring your parents if your blue card was issued after March 1st 2024.

## Should I book an appointment for all family members when applying for a visa?
Yes, please make sure all your family members are included in the appointment or book several ones, each for one family member, if they shall relocate with you.
The concrete procedure depends on the structure of the individual German embassy or external visa service which is responsible for you.

## Should I have travel insurance for obtaining my visa?
Yes, you should have travel insurance for getting your visa since this is one of the requirements to obtain your visa. The duration of the insurance should cover the trip and often embassies request one for the duration of the visa, which is at least 90 days.

## Do I need an apostille for my university degree?
No, an officially accepted translation to German or English is sufficient mostly. This needs to be done by a sworn translator which is certified. Often you find those listed on the embassy website. If your degree is bilingual or in English already this is fine as well and most embassies accept that.

## Do I need an apostille for the marriage certificate?
Yes, most embassies want next to the official translation of the marriage certificate an apostille as well. You get those from official authorities of your country of origin. The apostille will be needed at the foreigners department in Germany as well when you change the visa into a long term residence title. In general it is a good idea to have the marriage certificate translated to German as well as most embassies and authorities in germany want to see a German version.

## When can I expect my visa to be issued after the embassy interview takes place?
Usually after a few days or weeks. The embassy needs to check the data and request an approval from the foreigners department of destination. In work related visa cases the foreigners department has a deadline of 10 working days to refuse the case. If they don’t react within this timeframe the visa counts as approved. So often you have an answer within two weeks.

## How long will my visa be valid?
The visa will be valid for 90 days up to 12 months.
This time frame gives you enough time to travel to Germany and change your visa into a long term residence title, get your address registered at the city you will stay at and put a German health insurance in place. 
If you have a flight booked already please mention this during your interview and discuss with the officer that they are able to provide the visa in time.

## When my university degree is not listed in the official database Anabin, what should I do?
If your degree or your university are not listed in Anabin yet, you can apply for this via the German institution called kmk. This is a procedure we can assist with as well. The general fee from the kmk is about 208€ for proceeding the application. Here is a link to have a better understanding about the procedure and the needed documents for every country:
